# Analyze

## Summary of Analysis

This analysis compared TCS against Infosys, Wipro, and HCLTech across 21 fiscal
quarters (FY22 Q1 – FY27 Q1), focused on one core metric: the **decoupling gap**
— the difference between a company's revenue growth rate and its headcount
growth rate, quarter over quarter. A positive gap means revenue is growing
faster than headcount; a widening gap over time is the signature of a business
generating more output per employee added (or removed).

All four companies show a **positive shift in decoupling gap** from Period A
(FY22 TO FY24) to Period B (FY25 TO FY27 Q1) — except Wipro. This confirms a
real, sector-wide pattern consistent with a shift toward less headcount-dependent
revenue growth. But the magnitude and ranking of that shift is where the more
interesting, less obvious finding sits.

## Answering the Guiding Questions

### Q1: How do revenue, headcount, attrition, and margin trend per company?
All four companies grew revenue in both periods. TCS remains by far the largest
by absolute revenue (avg ₹54,842 Cr in Period A → ₹66,069 Cr in Period B) and
headcount (588,951 → 600,213), but its headcount growth nearly flattened in
Period B (avg headcount QoQ growth fell from 1.56% to -0.13%) while revenue
growth also slowed (2.77% → 1.88% QoQ) — both metrics decelerated together,
not just headcount. Infosys and HCLTech show a similar pattern of decelerating
headcount growth alongside continued (if slower) revenue growth. Attrition fell
across all four companies in Period B, suggesting a sector-wide cooling in
voluntary exits, not a TCS-specific retention story.

### Q2: Which company decoupled revenue from headcount fastest?
This is the headline finding, and it runs against the narrative the project
started from. Ranked by increase in average decoupling gap (Period A → Period B):

| Company | Period A gap | Period B gap | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| HCLTech | 0.93pp | 2.36pp | **+1.43pp** |
| Infosys | 1.28pp | 2.34pp | **+1.06pp** |
| TCS | 1.21pp | 2.01pp | **+0.80pp** |
| Wipro | 0.95pp | 0.68pp | **-0.27pp** |

TCS did decouple — its gap increased — but it decoupled *less* than both
HCLTech and Infosys. The "TCS is leading an AI-driven restructuring" framing
that motivated this project is only partially true: TCS is part of a real,
broader trend, but it is not the sector's front-runner in this specific metric.

### Q3: Is TCS an outlier, or is the whole peer set moving together?
The whole peer set is moving together, with one exception. Three of four
companies (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech) show a widening decoupling gap of similar
order of magnitude (+0.80pp to +1.43pp). Wipro is the true outlier here — the
only company where the gap *narrowed*. This suggests the underlying dynamic is
sector-wide (consistent with AI-assisted delivery, slower deal-driven hiring,
or macro caution), not a TCS-specific corporate decision, and that Wipro's
workforce/revenue relationship is behaving differently from its peers for
reasons this dataset alone can't isolate (worth a follow-up look at Wipro's
specific business mix or client concentration).

### Q4: Does margin expansion correlate more with headcount reduction or revenue growth?
Results are mixed by company, not uniform:
- **TCS, Infosys, Wipro** all show a **negative correlation** between margin
  change and headcount growth (-0.35, -0.43, -0.57 respectively) — consistent
  with, though not proof of, margin gains coinciding with quarters of slower
  headcount growth.
- **HCLTech** is the clear exception: near-zero correlation with headcount
  (-0.06) but a strong **positive** correlation with revenue growth (0.67) —
  its margin story appears to be driven by top-line growth, not headcount discipline.
- Despite this, HCLTech and Infosys's *average* EBIT margin actually **declined**
  slightly between periods (18.4%→17.7%, 21.6%→20.8%), even as their decoupling
  gaps widened — showing that a widening decoupling gap does not automatically
  translate into margin improvement.
- TCS held the highest, most stable margin of all four companies throughout
  (~24.6% in both periods) — its margin story is one of consistency, not expansion.

## Surprises
1. **TCS is not the standout decoupler** — this was the biggest surprise, and
   directly complicates the "TCS = AI productivity leader" narrative that
   motivated the project.
2. **Wipro moving in the opposite direction** from its three peers was
   unexpected and worth flagging as its own finding rather than noise.
3. **Widening decoupling gap does not reliably predict margin improvement** —
   two of the three companies with the largest gap increases (Infosys, HCLTech)
   saw margins move in the *opposite* direction.

## What the Data Does and Doesn't Show
The data shows a real, measurable, sector-wide shift toward revenue growth
outpacing headcount growth from FY25 onward, and shows that this shift is not
uniform across companies or cleanly tied to profitability. It does **not**
establish that AI specifically caused this shift — the timing is consistent
with (not proof of) an AI-productivity explanation, and alternative
explanations (post-COVID demand normalization, deal-pipeline softness,
deliberate cost discipline unrelated to AI) remain equally plausible given
this dataset alone.
