# Act

## Purpose

The Act phase translates the analytical findings into practical business
actions and identifies what additional information would be required before
making strategic workforce decisions.

The analysis identifies changes in the relationship between revenue growth and
headcount growth, but it does not establish the causes of those changes.

Therefore, the recommendations focus on **better workforce planning,
investigation, and measurement** rather than prescribing a specific level of
hiring or workforce reduction.

---

## Top Recommendations

### 1. Use revenue growth and workforce efficiency together when making hiring decisions

Track the following metrics together rather than using headcount growth alone
as an indicator of business expansion:

- Revenue QoQ growth
- Headcount QoQ growth
- Decoupling gap
- Revenue per employee
- EBIT margin
- Attrition

This provides a broader view of whether workforce expansion is occurring
alongside revenue growth and whether the relationship between the two is
changing.

**Business implication:**

Workforce planning can incorporate both growth requirements and efficiency
indicators instead of relying on headcount growth as a standalone measure of
business expansion.

---

### 2. Shift workforce planning from headcount volume toward skill mix and productivity

When revenue continues to grow without equivalent headcount growth,
organizations should evaluate whether future workforce requirements are
driven by:

- Critical technical skills
- Higher-value capabilities
- Employee utilization
- Automation
- AI-assisted workflows
- Delivery productivity
- Changes in service mix

This does not mean that lower hiring is automatically appropriate.

Instead, the objective is to determine **what types of capabilities are
required** and how workforce composition should evolve with business demand.

**Business implication:**

Hiring budgets can be evaluated based on required capabilities and expected
business demand rather than total employee volume alone.

---

### 3. Investigate the operational drivers behind the decoupling before making strategic decisions

The analysis identifies a changing relationship between revenue and headcount,
but it cannot determine the underlying cause.

Management should therefore investigate factors such as:

- Employee utilization
- Employee cost
- Revenue mix
- Pricing
- Service mix
- Subcontractor usage
- Acquisitions and divestitures
- Geographic mix
- Attrition by employee segment
- Automation adoption
- AI-assisted delivery
- Deal pipeline
- Client demand

These factors can help distinguish between different explanations for changes
in revenue per employee and the decoupling gap.

**Business implication:**

Organizations can avoid attributing changes in workforce efficiency to a
single factor without supporting operational evidence.

---

## Business Impact of Each Recommendation

| Recommendation | Potential Business Impact |
|---|---|
| **Use revenue growth and workforce efficiency together** | Provides a broader basis for workforce planning by linking hiring decisions to business growth, efficiency, profitability, and workforce indicators. |
| **Focus on skill mix and productivity** | Helps organizations evaluate which capabilities are required to support changing business demand rather than focusing only on total headcount. |
| **Investigate the drivers of decoupling** | Helps distinguish productivity-related changes from other factors such as demand, pricing, acquisitions, utilization, or workforce composition. |

## Connection to the Analysis

The selected peer group shows that revenue and headcount growth did not move
together in the same way during Period B.

TCS's average decoupling gap increased from **1.21 pp in Period A to 2.01 pp
in Period B**, an improvement of **+0.80 pp**.

HCLTech and Infosys showed larger improvements, while Wipro moved in the
opposite direction.

This indicates that the observed change was not unique to TCS, but the
magnitude and direction differed across companies.

The appropriate business response is therefore not simply to increase or
reduce hiring.

Instead, organizations should investigate what is driving the changing
relationship between revenue and workforce size and align future workforce
decisions with the underlying business and operational requirements.
